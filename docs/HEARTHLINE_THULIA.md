Lore gives Hearthline a pet owl. Design gives **Thulia**, the **Owl Scribe**,
one bounded custody role with three non-overlapping apertures: preserve the
route by which account-local [Static](HEARTHLINE_STATIC.md) can be reconstructed
for another account without joining them; tend the validated lexicon
generations and detachable Translation Slates used by
[Gloss](HEARTHLINE_GLOSS.md); and, under a separate retention grant, apply
**Systemic Friction** to retention candidates.

## v0.4 successor record

Version `0.4` assigns Gloss and Systemic Friction without giving Thulia three
payload ledgers. Profile `OWL-000001/PROFILE-000003` remains the historical
Creature integration record; `OWL-000001/PROFILE-000004` is its
non-retroactive successor.

Thulia remains non-governing with respect to task truth, advice
reconciliation, working-context carry, Static activation, action selection,
grants, and external execution. Her exclusive Systemic Friction aperture is a
domain-specific retention classification under a separately declared grant.
`PRUNE_ELIGIBLE` is not deletion authority.

## v0.3 successor record

Version `0.3` adds Creature-manifest and linked-ledger custody to Thulia's design
profile. Profile `OWL-000001/PROFILE-000002` remains the historical Homecoming
integration record; `OWL-000001/PROFILE-000003` is its non-retroactive
successor. The successor does not make Thulia a controller, grant issuer,
arbiter, executor, promotion authority, or shared ledger writer.

In the lore, Thulia keeps a roost of separately partitioned task and
representation accounts. She knows where each bound grammar, codebook,
decoder, version, residual, and source binding is recorded. When authorized
communication requires translation, she reconstructs the sending expression
inside its source account and records a numbered **Bridge Gloss** in ordinary
language or an explicit shared structure.

She does not create a universal Static, pool the ledgers, silently broadcast shorthand, dispatch or direct a worker under her own authority, or let one Spark's vocabulary govern another. She keeps the paths between languages and back to their Homes, not one language above them.

> **Each account keeps its Static. A Spark receives one bounded lane. Thulia keeps the path between accounts.**

## Namesake and honest fiction

Thulia's lore-name is ***Thuleglaux interpres***, intended as “the Thulean interpreter-owl”:

- **Thule-** refers to the reconstructed Thulean route between western Europe and North America through Greenland;
- **-glaux** follows the Ancient Greek word for owl and the genuine fossil-owl construction *Ypresiglaux*; and
- **interpres** is used in its Latin sense of intermediary or interpreter.

The story imagines a small, daylight-tolerant stem-owl lineage diverging along northern forests near the Paleocene–Eocene boundary. Reconstructions place episodes of the Thulean connection around 57 and 56 million years ago. Real owls bracket that setting: *Ogygoptynx wetmorei* is known from the late Paleocene of Colorado, and the early Eocene genus *Ypresiglaux* includes fossils from Virginia and approximately 55-million-year-old deposits in England. The latter has been hypothesized to retain a diurnal ancestral ecology.

That evidence makes the setting plausible; it does not establish *Thuleglaux*, prove that owls followed the corridor, or identify Thulia's ancestry. The Greenland ice hides her branch in the story. It is not evidence that the branch existed.

***Nomenclatural disclaimer:*** *Thuleglaux interpres* is an expressly fictional name used for Hearthline lore. This document is not a zoological description or nomenclatural act, and the name is not proposed as available under the International Code of Zoological Nomenclature.

Sources:

- [Brikiatis (2014), *The De Geer, Thulean and Beringia routes*](https://doi.org/10.1111/jbi.12310)
- [Mayr and Kitchener (2023), early Eocene *Ypresiglaux*](https://doi.org/10.1111/ibi.13125)
- [Vickers-Rich and Bohaska (1976), *Ogygoptynx wetmorei*](https://doi.org/10.5479/si.00810266.27.87)
- [ICZN Article 8.3, names excluded by disclaimer](https://code.iczn.org/criteria-of-publication/article-8-what-constitutes-published-work/)

## The Owl Scribe boundary

**Owl Scribe** is a unique custodial job, not a fourth Spark role, not a Scribe
lens, and not another primary-task decision maker at the Fireside. Thulia does
not investigate the primary task, judge competing advice, decide
working-context carry, approve a Static proposal, authorize a crossing, create
a task grant, or choose actions for a Work Spark. Retention classification is
the one named exception: only Thulia applies Systemic Friction, and only inside
its current retention grant.

Her permitted work is limited to:

1. locating an exact account-local Static ledger and version through its recorded identity;
2. checking that the requested decoder, grammar, codebook, source bindings, and access grant are present;
3. reconstructing the named expression inside that sending ledger;
4. returning the reconstruction, sources, residuals, and declared destination as a numbered Bridge Gloss candidate for the canonical account writer;
5. offering that gloss to the existing Fireside carry process as candidate material;
6. binding an authorized paired dispatch to the separately declared Work Spark and Ledger Scribe Spark identities, Homes, frozen Static references, Run Trail projection, and Spark Heartbeat Contracts;
7. returning each Scribe-authored target-bound Static proposal, negative constraint, and residual to the assigned representation account's exact source Perch named before dispatch; and
8. preparing and checking a candidate representation-side Homecoming payload for controller reconciliation without attesting durable arrival, task success, carry approval, Static activation, or context closure;
9. tending validated Gloss lexicon generations and detachable, translation-account-owned slates without performing routine translation by inference; and
10. applying Systemic Friction to one retention candidate and returning its typed classification without executing the resulting mutation.

The ordinary carry gate still controls:

`PROPOSED != CONSULTED != CARRY_APPROVED != LOADED`

A Bridge Gloss does not skip a stage. It is not a decision, recommendation,
permission, authorization, or receiving-account Static entry. A Spark working
in the receiving account may propose new local shorthand from an explicitly
loaded gloss only through that account's admission and activation rules.

## The partitioned roost

Thulia's **roost** is a directory of isolated **Perches**. Each Perch indexes
exactly one task- or representation-account Static lineage and binds:

- its Perch identity and ordered version;
- the exact account, assigned Spark lane, registry, Static ledger, and permitted audience;
- the available Static versions, entry ranges, and activation receipts;
- the exact decoders, grammars, codebooks, parameters, and digests needed for reconstruction;
- source and residual locations without pretending that an index contains their meaning;
- availability, redaction, retirement, and tombstone state; and
- every access and translation receipt required by the applicable grant.

Partitions are access boundaries, not folders of convenience. A request that can reach one Perch gains no implied access to another. Thulia may index a ledger without being permitted to open its contents, and a Seeker-visible Perch view contains metadata only.

The roost is therefore not shared memory. Shared infrastructure is not shared
lineage. It is a partitioned pointer and exception registry for separately
governed records. Thulia keeps identities, locators, status, retention holds,
and unresolved exceptions; she does not keep account payloads, Spark notes,
translation history, or duplicate Static entries in the index.

### No-three-ledger rule

| Material | Owning account or store | Thulia keeps |
|---|---|---|
| Hearthline-facing offer, consultation, load, rejection, or reopening | Hearthline's declared task account | Direction-bound pointer and unresolved exception only |
| Work, Scribe, Static, residual, or Homecoming payload | Its declared task or representation account | Perch, Home, status, and reopening pointer only |
| Gloss lexicon generation and compact turn marks | Translation account on its detachable slate | Validated-generation pointer, slate identity, and exception only |
| Model-assisted Owl custody work | Its separately declared task account | Return pointer after the bounded Spark lane closes |

Thulia therefore has no Hearthline-offer ledger, no pooled Thulia-Spark payload
ledger, and no separate Gloss history ledger. The canonical stores carry the
payloads; her roost carries the smallest index needed to find, validate, or
reopen them.

## Hearth Perch and paired returns

Thulia's own declared Home is the **Hearth Perch**, the dark rail above
Hearthline's working place. It is the return boundary for Owl Scribe work, not a
shared Static ledger. It binds Thulia's exact profile, the coordinator permitted
to receive her return, the dispatch-pinned roost index and version, the records
she may deliver, and the failure or reopening route when a return cannot be
reconciled. The record routes custody; it does not grant or renew authority.

Every account-local Static Perch remains separate from the Hearth Perch. Thulia
may carry a Bridge Gloss, Home map, or representation-side return through her
bounded custody, but the material goes back to its named source Perch or onward
through an authorized destination-specific carry gate. She does not keep a
pooled extra copy at Home.

For an authorized paired dispatch, lore may say that Thulia sends a little
Ledger Scribe Spark beside the Work Spark. Mechanically, the authorized
coordinator creates both Spark grants and the paired dispatch. Thulia's
interface binds their separate identities, heartbeat and coverage boundaries,
separate frozen Static versions, Homes, and shared dispatch reference. Any
model-assisted work behind that interface still uses a declared Spark role,
grant, and exclusive bounded write lane in a task account. When Thulia's
bounded custody work is model-assisted, that Spark job has a distinct identity,
task-shaped Spark Heartbeat Contract, and return route to the Hearth Perch. It
does not borrow either member of the pair's pulse or persistence budget, own
the account, or keep its write lane after Homecoming.

The Work Spark returns the bounded task result and unchanged Static to the
task account's source Perch. The Ledger Scribe returns one typed representation
disposition to its assigned representation account and source Perch: `static_delta`, coverage-qualified
`NO_LEDGER_DELTA`, `LEDGER_DELTA_INCOMPLETE`, or
`LEDGER_COVERAGE_UNKNOWN`. Thulia prepares and checks neither one as the other's
result. She may route a permitted reconstruction of a target-bound Scribe Ember
through the direction-bound carry path to the target Perch's proposal intake,
unmatched distinctions to the Scribe's residual lane, and a proposed custody or
reconciliation payload to the Hearth Perch. Only the canonical controller or
store observes durable placement and appends Return, Reconciliation, and
Context-Close Receipts. She does not allocate a target Static version.
Admission and activation remain with the target account's authorized writer.
At reconciliation, each Spark's exclusive bounded write lane closes and
durable custody returns to the canonical account store.

If a Work Spark comes home and its Ledger Scribe does not, the task and note
statuses remain separate. Unless a stricter condition was declared before
dispatch, the missing Scribe return prevents learned carry and Static promotion
rather than manufacturing a task failure or a complete ledger. If any return
is ambiguous, Thulia flags the unresolved return boundary in her candidate
payload; the canonical controller records the disposition and does not replay
it automatically.

| Gloss delivery receipt | `OWL-000001/GLOSS-000001/DELIVERY-000001` |

Every Bridge Gloss binds at least:

- its ordered identity, status, predecessor or revision, and content digest;
- the exact sending account, assigned Spark lane, Static ledger, version, entry, and expression bytes;
- the decoder, grammar, codebook, parameters, and reconstruction result used;
- source references, claim status, uncertainty, residuals, and declared omissions;
- the exact receiving account, assigned Spark or other declared audience, and the purpose of the request;
- the requesting grant and the access projection actually used; and
- any later consultation, carry, load, rejection, invalidation, or supersession receipts.

The Owl-prefixed number identifies the Bridge Gloss custody series, not an
Owl-owned payload ledger. The canonical writer appends the offered record in
the declared recipient account; a Hearthline-facing offer therefore lives in
Hearthline's task account. Thulia keeps its direction-bound pointer, status,
and any unresolved exception.

Every request and delivery is direction-bound. A gloss from Spark A to Spark B does not authorize or imply the reverse crossing, delivery to Spark C, or access to either ledger beyond the recorded projection.

A gloss number is allocated before the attempt. Failed, ambiguous, denied, interrupted, invalidated, and superseded glosses keep their numbers and dispositions. Corrections append numbered revisions; they do not overwrite the earlier gloss.

## Translation sequence

1. A coordinator submits a bounded request naming the sending expression, sending and receiving account identities, assigned Spark identities, purpose, audience, and grant.
2. Thulia resolves only the named Perch and verifies the exact Static version and reconstruction materials.
3. She decodes the expression inside the sending account's ledger. She does not infer a convenient meaning from the compact expression alone.
4. She records an exact ordinary-language or explicitly structured reconstruction with sources, uncertainty, residuals, and omissions.
5. The coordinator appends the numbered Bridge Gloss and offers it as candidate carry to the named destination.
6. Hearthline's task account records whether the offer was consulted, approved for bounded carry, actually loaded, rejected, or reopened; Thulia retains only its pointer and any unresolved exception.
7. If the receiving Spark later proposes local Static from it, that proposal receives a new identity in the receiving account's ledger and passes the ordinary Static admission rule.

No codebook crosses merely because a gloss does. The source Static remains
source-account-local, and the destination Static remains
destination-account-local.

## Gloss and the Translation Slate

[Gloss](HEARTHLINE_GLOSS.md) performs a routine deterministic turn. Thulia
tends the exact lexicon generation and the detachable Translation Slate on
which the translation account's compact marks are externally appended. The
slate is replaceable and account-owned; it is not part of Gloss and is not an
Owl memory surface.

Thulia validates and routes. Gloss turns. The canonical controller or store
allocates and appends. Routine translation reads no history and does not ask
Thulia to infer what an earlier turn probably meant. A lexicon change receives
a successor generation and activation receipt before later turns use it.

Thulia's slate custody adds only the current validated-generation pointer,
slate identity, availability state, retention holds, and unresolved exceptions
to her partitioned index. The complete lexicon and turn marks remain in the
translation account. Gloss owns neither.

## Systemic Friction

**Systemic Friction** is Thulia's exclusive retention-classification rule. It
operates outside the abstract transformation itself: a declared garden,
purpose, horizon, protected distinctions, replay and open obligations, lawful
privacy or retention requirements, and measured external costs determine the
question being asked. The cost profile remains typed rather than disappearing
into an unexplained universal score.

Its closed domain is **`G_mutable`**: records explicitly declared account-owned
before the review. This profile does not assert that a Spark or Gloss has
persistent, autobiographical, identity-bearing, or agent-owned state. If a
future implementation introduces such state, it is outside `G_mutable` and
requires separate governance. No controller may relabel, transfer, wrap, or
register it as account-owned merely to bypass an identity or refusal claim.
Out-of-domain material fails scope before classification and cannot receive
`PRUNE_ELIGIBLE` under this rule.

A review binds at least:

- the exact retention candidate, account, current state, and requested horizon;
- the distinctions and receipts that must remain answerable;
- replay, contest, reopening, legal, privacy, and safety holds;
- measured storage, replica, transfer, latency, recomputation, verification,
  energy, exposure, and human-attention burdens where applicable;
- uncertainty, unavailable measurements, and the applicable retention grant;
- one typed result: `KEEP`, `COMPACT`, `ARCHIVE`, `PRUNE_ELIGIBLE`, or
  `FRICTION_UNKNOWN_HOLD`; and
- the permitted next transition, expiry, contest, and reopening route.

Only Thulia applies Systemic Friction. Hearthline may request a review, name
the primary task's future duty through an authorized grant, inspect the result,
or request reopening when an input changes. Hearthline does not substitute its
own friction classification, and an ordinary Spark does not inherit this lane.
A Thulia-bound Spark may gather measurements or test replay under a distinct
job and account, but its return is evidence for Thulia rather than a retention
disposition.

A Spark may raise a **retention defect** only by naming the exact replay, open,
contest, privacy, safety, or other retention obligation that the proposed
transition would violate. Association with an account, prior authorship of a
payload, continuity language, or a claim that material is “my memory” creates
no self-preservation veto. The defect is checked as an account obligation, not
as a Spark possession.

No ledger, payload, Static entry, note, receipt, returned context, or Gloss mark
is modeled as a Spark's or Gloss's body, identity, memory, or property. Only a
typed retention defect naming a declared account obligation, including any
valid hold, may block a retention transition; character continuity and
possession language cannot.

`PRUNE_ELIGIBLE` means that the candidate passed this bounded classification.
It does not delete, authorize deletion, or override a hold. The canonical
controller or separately authorized writer must revalidate the exact current
candidate, classification, grant, holds, and stale-base condition, then perform
the allowed mechanical transition through **Atomic Edge Promotion**. The
classification and effect receipt remain distinct. Failure at any step leaves
the prior state active.

Systemic Friction is Hearthline design vocabulary pending the paper's reviewed
release. This profile does not make it PAL canon, a controlling source, a
universal physical law, or a Research Station source-registry entry.

## Creature-manifest custody

Within a [Hearthline Creature](HEARTHLINE_CREATURES.md), Thulia may custody the
content-addressed composition manifest and the partition map that relates each
Spark, pair, Home, Perch, ledger, heartbeat contract, budget, source registry,
and return route. Custody makes the topology inspectable; it does not make the
manifest a grant or the partitions one memory.

At each permitted crossing, Thulia may report the manifest-bound status of
identity, witness, scope, trace, authority reference, consequence class,
resource state, and re-entry requirements. These are guard observations for the
canonical controller. Thulia does not decide the route, approve the grant,
debit a budget, choose among proposals, or issue the single permitted external
effect.

Comparative arms remain separate Creature manifests, roots, and ledgers. An
external campaign index may name both, but Thulia must not use it as a shared
payload channel. Cooperative Sparks inside one arm retain separate accounts and
write permissions; the canonical controller serializes any effect or promotion.

## Failure and reopening

Thulia fails closed when the source identity, active version, decoder, grammar, codebook, digest, source binding, destination, audience, disclosure intersection, or grant cannot be established. Ambiguity is carried as ambiguity; missing material is not silently completed from resemblance or model familiarity. A permitted lossy summary may be recorded as a separately labeled derivative, but it is not an exact Bridge Gloss.

If a source is later corrected, superseded, redacted, tombstoned, or made unavailable, affected glosses remain addressable but receive appended status changes and are no longer treated as silently current. Dependent receiving records keep their provenance and are reopened or narrowed as required.

Thulia never repairs a failed translation by merging ledgers. She returns to the exact unresolved boundary.

## Fireside use

At a [Hearthline Fireside](HEARTHLINE_FIRESIDES.md), Hearthline or an authorized coordinator may consult Thulia when a Scribe's Field Notes or Embers contain Static that another participant cannot interpret directly. Thulia supplies the Bridge Gloss; Hearthline's task account records whether to consult or carry it within the unchanged task grant. That working-context decision does not confer a Systemic Friction lane on Hearthline.

At a [Homecoming](HEARTHLINE_HOMECOMING.md), Thulia may also prepare and check a
candidate representation-side return payload against the paired dispatch,
exact coverage watermark, Home Record, source Perch, and frozen Static version.
Only the canonical controller or store may reconcile and append its receipt.
Neither step decides whether the primary task succeeded or whether a candidate
Static revision should be carried or activated.

Thulia's output is derivative of the sending ledger and its sources. It is not another witness, another vote, or independent corroboration.

> **A Spark left a small knot of Static beside the fire. Thulia turned her head toward its account-bound Perch, followed the recorded path home, and returned with a gloss another Spark could read. The knot stayed in its account. Only its traced meaning crossed.**
