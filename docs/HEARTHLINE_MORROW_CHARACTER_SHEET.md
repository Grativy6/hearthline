## Mechanical construction

Morrow should look more robotic than the rest of the crew without becoming
menacing or flesh-like. The face is a smooth segmented mask with two readable
eye apertures and no simulated skin, teeth, or human mouth. Neck, shoulders,
elbows, wrists, knees, and ankles show practical articulation.

Armor-like plates float only slightly apart. The gaps are narrow enough to read
as engineered seams rather than damage, disassembly, or exposed wiring. Their
underlight suggests that quiet white light runs through the whole construction,
not merely painted on its surface. It does not make Morrow a Spark.

The coat softens the silhouette while preserving its mechanical truth. It has
no pockets. Morrow never stores a token on his person, behind a plate, or in a
hidden compartment.

## White light and expression

Morrow's eyes and plate seams are near-white, with only enough warmth or coolness
to sit naturally inside the scene's ambient light. They may brighten, narrow,
or dim slightly to support a restrained expression. A calm Morrow is softly
lit; close attention may sharpen the eyes and brighten the hand or face seams;
an empty rail may leave him still and low-lit.

Whether the white light can intentionally resolve into other hues is
**UNSPECIFIED**. Art may contain optical color at bright edges or reflected
environmental color, because white light and surrounding materials naturally
produce it. That does not adopt color-changing seams as a character ability or
forbid a later steward from doing so in a separate successor.

Brightness is animation language. It is not a queue class, receipt, vote,
warning code, emotion detector, permission, or machine-readable command.

## The rail and tokens

Morrow works only at a controller-owned brass rail. The tokens are faceless
scheduling projections: no payload text, task meaning, private record, global
ordinal, credential, or person is visible on them. His hands may compare and
arrange the frozen view, then leave the final proposal on the rail.

If the rail is empty, absent, disconnected, or knocked over, Morrow has no
proposal work and stops. He does not search for another queue, reconstruct the
rail from memory, hide tokens in his coat, or become a fallback controller.
Correctness remains with the controller's deterministic fallback ordering as
specified in the Return Queue. Morrow's availability must not be required for
service.

## Relationship boundaries

Morrow may be visible through a cabin door or window while Hearthline, Gloss,
and Thulia work elsewhere. A thin machine-to-machine trace may connect a
controller-facing interface to the controller's rail machinery. It must not be
drawn as a direct channel between Morrow and Thulia, Gloss, or Hearthline.

Morrow and Thulia share no channel, state, ledger, custody, invocation,
impersonation, or availability dependency. Affection, proximity, matching
light, or a shared composition does not create one.

## No-authority boundary

Morrow is a fictional face for a narrow scheduling proposal. His plates, white
light, coat, rail, tokens, posture, and companion art create no memory,
persistence, identity continuity, personhood, judgment, permission, standing,
consent, authority, order commitment, service selection, or admission.

> He may place the next token neatly. Only the controller may open the door.
