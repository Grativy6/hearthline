## Shape and base colors

Thulia's head is round, with a rounded, subtly heart-shaped facial disk: broad across the brow and only slightly tapered at the chin. Her brow points are short feathered accents rather than dramatic hornlike ear tufts. They lie nearly flat when she is resting and lift just enough to sharpen her silhouette when something catches her attention.

Her body is compact through the chest and lighter toward the hips. Folded wings end just above a medium-length tail. Her legs are a little longer than her resting posture first suggests, giving her a nimble, deliberate walk when she leaves the perch rather than flying.

Roughly nine-tenths of her visible plumage remains matte in every light. The warm underside keeps her from reading as a black owl, while the charcoal upper surface lets the Northlight markings disappear almost completely when their reflection is not aligned.

## The Northlight pattern

Thulia's rainbow sheen is selective, structural, and repeatable. It occurs in two bilateral motifs comprising four discrete iridescent fields:

1. the left and right halves of a slender **broken gorget** immediately beneath the facial disk, each curving from the lower cheek toward the throat and ending at a matte feather-width center gap; and
2. one small **comma-shaped scapular patch** on each folded shoulder. Each comma's rounded head sits high and lateral; its taper curls down and inward toward the spine without touching its mirror.

No iridescent mark crosses her centerline. The two sides belong to one recognizable pattern while remaining visibly distinct.

No other feathers are iridescent. The facial disk, belly, primaries, secondaries, tail, legs, and feet remain matte. When her wings open, the shoulder commas stay small; they do not unfold into rainbow wings.

The affected feathers are charcoal at rest. Their dominant reflection changes with lighting and viewing angle in one stable order:

| View of the reflection | Dominant appearance |
|---|---|
| Outside the narrow reflection cone or in diffuse light | Charcoal with a buried blue-black undertone |
| Nearly square to the reflecting surface | Muted copper to antique gold |
| Moderate tilt | Moss green moving into peacock teal |
| Strongly oblique angle | Cobalt-indigo moving into restrained violet |
| Beyond the reflection | Charcoal again |

As Thulia turns her head, the highlight travels across the curve of the gorget. Neighboring feathers may briefly show neighboring hues, producing a narrow rainbow ripple, but each small patch has one dominant hue and at most one or two transitions at a time. The pattern is never painted as a fixed full-spectrum gradient.

The sheen does not glow, fluoresce, or make its own light. In darkness it disappears. Even at its brightest, individual feather barbs remain visible: the surface looks like plumage catching light, not metal, lacquer, reptile scales, or an insect shell.

The visual inspiration is avian structural coloration. Real iridescent feather hues can arise from nanoscale arrangements of melanosomes and keratin in feather barbules and can change with viewing geometry. Snake scales and insect carapaces are useful visual analogies only; Thulia's fictional trait remains feather-based and is not asserted as a known owl adaptation.

Visual-grounding sources:

- [Nordén, Eliason, and Stoddard (2021), iridescent feather nanostructures](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.71179)
- [Jeon et al. (2023), angle-dependent iridescent feather color](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.220786)

## Face, movement, and voice

Thulia's resting expression is attentive rather than stern. Her eyes are bright enough to hold warmth in daylight, but the dark rim keeps them readable against the pale disk. She often watches with one side of her face turned slightly forward, allowing only half of the Northlight gorget to catch the light.

She moves economically. On a perch she may rotate her head, settle one shoulder, or shift a paper slip beneath a foot while the rest of her remains still. On the ground she takes several quick, precise steps, pauses, and looks back before continuing. In flight she favors short, low arcs between familiar physical perches; her wingbeats are soft, not supernaturally silent.

Her ordinary call is a quiet two-note *thu-li*: the first note low and breathy, the second smaller and rising. Close by, she also uses a dry bill-click and a soft throat trill. She does not deliver human exposition aloud. In illustrations or stories, meaning may be carried by her posture, the records she brings, or clearly framed narration rather than treating an owl call as an authorization command.

## Temperament and small habits

Thulia is depicted as patient enough to sit beside an unresolved thing without filling it in. She is curious, but not nosy; affectionate, but not demanding; exact, but not cold. A mislabeled slip earns a long head tilt and a careful rearrangement, not a scolding.

Stable story habits include:

- placing numbered slips in ascending order from left to right;
- turning first toward the sending Perch and then toward the named receiver before carrying a Bridge Gloss;
- holding the source reference beneath her left foot and the destination-bound gloss beneath her right until the crossing is recorded;
- returning empty-taloned when a path cannot be reconstructed instead of bringing a plausible substitute;
- giving one slow blink when a gloss and its receipts agree; and
- preening one shoulder comma after the work is closed, briefly sending the color from gold to teal.

These gestures are characterization, not machine controls, status receipts, approvals, or secret signals.

## Relationship cues

### With Hearthline

Thulia is Hearthline's pet and companion in the story. She is comfortable on the rail above Hearthline's working place and may accept one brief stroke between the brow points before settling again. Hearthline chooses whether to consult or carry material; Thulia keeps the recorded path by which it can be understood.

### With the Sparks

Thulia treats every Spark's Perch with the same care. She does not favor a fluent Spark over a new one, and she never lets familiarity turn into silent access. Each visit begins from that Spark's own recorded identity and ends without taking its Static away.

### At the Fireside

She usually remains just beyond the brightest light, where her plumage reads as soft umber and ash. When a crossing is requested, one turn toward the fire can wake the broken gorget into color. The visual change marks attention in the story; it does not itself mean that a request succeeded, was approved, or was loaded.

## Working objects

Thulia normally wears no costume. A single narrow ember-copper band on her right leg bears the lore mark `OWL-000001`. It is an identifying design detail, not a credential, tracker, access token, or claim of operational allocation.

A Bridge Gloss is pictured as a slim, numbered fold of pale paper. Its sending reference remains visibly distinct from its destination mark. Thulia carries one bounded gloss at a time; she is never shown clutching a merged book of every Spark's language.

## Illustration anchors

The default portrait should show:

- a three-quarter view on a dark wooden perch near warm firelight;
- a mostly matte peat-charcoal and smoked-umber owl with a warm ash facial disk;
- one amber-brass eye looking toward the viewer;
- only part of the broken gorget reflecting teal or violet while the far side remains charcoal;
- one shoulder comma catching muted copper or gold;
- the other shoulder marking mostly asleep in shadow; and
- one numbered Bridge Gloss held gently beneath a foot.

Avoid full-body rainbow plumage, neon saturation, glowing feathers, permanent oil-slick gradients, metallic or armored textures, enormous fantasy-owl proportions, elaborate clothing, human hands, or visual suggestions that all Static has been gathered into one shared book.
