## Cartoon construction

Thulia should remain recognizable from her unadorned silhouette.

Use a round underlying head shape. The crest plumes sit above that circle and may change pose without changing the size or structure of her skull. They can read at a glance like small cat ears, but they are feather groups, not mammalian ears, horns, or large owl ear tufts.

A stable animation construction uses:

- a standing height of approximately `2.35–2.50` head diameters, excluding unusually raised crests;
- a compact body no wider than the head at rest;
- a facial disk occupying most of the head without becoming a flat mask;
- large forward-facing eyes kept on one stable horizontal axis in neutral poses;
- a small central hooked beak whose upper base remains anchored between the eyes;
- broad folded wings ending shortly above the tail;
- slightly long legs that become more apparent when she walks; and
- simplified feather groups rather than individually simulated photoreal feathers.

Her head may squash or stretch slightly during fast animation, but the facial disk, eye spacing, beak root, brow branch points, and crest roots return to the same construction. One pose should never make her look like a different species or a human face fitted inside an owl.

Avoid realistic skin motion, exposed humanlike lips, teeth, mammalian ears, a flexible rubber face, or a photoreal owl forced through human expressions.

## Facial disk, brows, and crests

Thulia's expression system has three coordinated layers:

1. eyes and lids;
2. the **matte brow-boundary**; and
3. the two movable crest plumes.

The brow-boundary is the visible feather boundary where the dark crown meets the pale facial disk. It begins above the inner portion of each eye, curves across the brow, and turns outward toward the corresponding crest root. It is not a separate human eyebrow and should never appear as a floating line, skin fold, or iridescent stripe.

At the outer brow, the feather flow branches into one left and one right crest plume. Each crest may be drawn with a few tightly grouped feather tips, but it remains one silhouette control and one Northlight field per side.

The brow-boundary remains matte at every angle. The crest feathers beyond the branch point carry the iridescent field.

### Expression grammar

| State | Eyes and lids | Matte brow-boundary | Crest plumes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resting warmth | Open, soft focus | Shallow neutral curve | Low and gently swept back |
| Curious | One eye or head angle leads | Slight asymmetry | One plume raised or rotated outward |
| Focused | Pupils steady | Both curves angle inward | Slightly forward and alert |
| Concerned | Inner boundary lifts | Soft upward bend | Lowered and angled outward |
| Amused | One lid lowers slightly | One side relaxes | One plume cocked |
| Surprised | Eyes widen | Curves lift without leaving the disk | Both rise |
| Still or listening | Eyes softly closed or narrowed | Neutral | Flattened close to the round silhouette |
| Uncertain | Unequal focus or head tilt | Deliberately unequal curves | Different heights or angles |

Symmetry may support steadiness or settled attention. Asymmetry may support curiosity, doubt, reconsideration, or mild mischief. These are animation cues, not a codebook, authorization language, diagnostic system, or claim that a particular internal state exists.

Crest motion changes the angle of the iridescent feather surface. A raised left crest and lowered right crest may therefore show different Northlight colors under the same light. That visible difference is expected.

## Talking beak

The cartoon beak may deform enough to support speech without making a realistic owl appear anatomically broken.

Use a small set of repeatable shapes:

- closed;
- slight part;
- small speaking opening;
- rounded open call;
- broad emphasized opening; and
- a restrained softened corner shape for warmth or amusement.

The upper beak keeps its central hooked identity. Most opening motion belongs to the lower mandible and the soft feathered corners around the beak. Mild squash, bend, and corner movement are permitted; human lips, teeth, broad mammalian smiles, or a beak that migrates away from the facial center are not.

Eye, brow, crest, head, and body motion should carry most emotion. The beak supports the performance rather than becoming a human mouth.

Thulia's depicted speech remains story presentation. Her ordinary lore voice is the two-note *thu-li*, a bill-click, and a soft throat trill; no pose or mouth shape is an operational instruction or authorization command.

## Bilateral identity and copper-band rule

**Right and left always mean Thulia's anatomical right and left, never the viewer's.**

Thulia wears exactly one narrow ember-copper band. It encircles her anatomical right lower leg at the feather-to-scale transition, just above the foot. Her anatomical left leg is unbanded.

| Required view | Orientation | Copper-band placement |
|---|---|---|
| Front | Thulia faces the viewer | On image-left, which is Thulia's anatomical right |
| Back | Thulia faces away | On image-right, which is Thulia's anatomical right |
| Anatomical-right profile | Thulia's right side faces the viewer; beak points image-left | Clearly visible on the near leg |
| Anatomical-left profile | Thulia's left side faces the viewer; beak points image-right | On the far anatomical-right leg; partly visible only if the pose permits |
| Rear wing-open view | Thulia faces away | On image-right if the legs are shown |
| Three-quarter view | Determined from her body, not screen position | Remains on the same anatomical right leg through rotation |

Every turnaround should carry small `R` and `L` anatomical markers beneath the feet. A generic panel labeled only `SIDE` is insufficient for the controlling turnaround.

A mirrored image is not automatically another valid view. Mirroring swaps anatomical sides, reverses the band, and changes the local viewing angles of the six Northlight fields. A corrected mirror must be reconstructed and recorded as a new image rather than silently flipped.

The copper band is a visual identity detail bearing the lore mark `OWL-000001`. It is not a credential, tracker, access token, grant, or operational allocation.

## The six-field Northlight pattern

Northlight is selective structural iridescence. It occupies exactly six bilateral fields:

| Field label | Anatomical placement |
|---|---|
| `NL-CREST-R` | The feathered portion of the anatomical-right crest beyond its matte brow branch point |
| `NL-CREST-L` | The feathered portion of the anatomical-left crest beyond its matte brow branch point |
| `NL-GORGET-R` | Anatomical-right half of the broken gorget beneath the facial disk |
| `NL-GORGET-L` | Anatomical-left half of the broken gorget beneath the facial disk |
| `NL-SHOULDER-R` | Small comma-shaped patch on the anatomical-right scapular region |
| `NL-SHOULDER-L` | Small comma-shaped patch on the anatomical-left scapular region |

These labels are drawing and rigging references only. They are not operational identities, memory partitions, signals, or permissions.

### Field geometry

The two crest fields begin only after the matte facial-disk boundary branches into the raised plume. The crown, crest roots, and brow-boundary remain matte. Lowering a crest does not erase its field; it turns the reflective surface away until it reads charcoal.

The gorget remains broken at the center. Each half curves from the lower cheek toward the throat and ends at least one matte feather-width from its mirror.

Each shoulder comma has a rounded head high and lateral on the scapular region. Its taper curls down and inward without reaching the spine or touching the other comma. When the wings open, both commas remain small and proximal. They do not spread onto the primaries, secondaries, tail, or belly.

No Northlight field crosses Thulia's centerline. No seventh field is implied.

### Angle-dependent color

All six fields share one material behavior, but each field resolves color from its own surface angle relative to the viewer and light.

| View of a field's reflection | Dominant appearance |
|---|---|
| Outside the reflection cone or under diffuse light | Charcoal with a buried blue-black undertone |
| Nearly square to the reflecting surface | Muted copper to antique gold |
| Moderate tilt | Moss green moving into peacock teal |
| Strongly oblique angle | Cobalt-indigo moving into restrained violet |
| Beyond the reflection | Charcoal again |

Different fields may show different colors simultaneously because their feather surfaces face different directions. The two crests are especially variable: moving one plume changes its local angle and may move it from gold to green, teal, indigo, violet, or sleeping charcoal while the other plume keeps another phase.

This color change follows geometry, not mood. A crest does not become blue because Thulia is sad or gold because she approves. If neither Thulia, the viewer, nor the light changes angle, the Northlight colors should remain stable.

Within one field, use one dominant hue and at most one or two neighboring transitions. Never paint a permanent full-spectrum gradient across every field. At rest, the affected feathers return visually to charcoal.

Northlight does not glow, fluoresce, pulse, make its own light, behave like a screen, or become metal, lacquer, reptile scales, armor, glass, or an insect shell. Individual feather barbs remain visible at the brightest reflection.

The visual grounding remains avian structural coloration:

- [Nordén, Eliason, and Stoddard (2021), iridescent feather nanostructures](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.71179)
- [Jeon et al. (2023), angle-dependent iridescent feather color](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.220786)

These sources support the visual mechanism generally. They do not establish Thulia, *Thuleglaux*, or iridescent crest feathers in a real owl lineage.

## Movement

Thulia moves economically even in cartoon form. Her body may remain nearly still while her eyes, brow-boundary, crests, or head angle carry the action.

Stable movement cues include:

- a precise head turn before a body turn;
- a small anticipatory crest lift before taking flight;
- short, low flights between familiar perches;
- several quick deliberate steps followed by a complete pause;
- a slow blink when a gloss and its receipts agree;
- one-crest asymmetry when something remains unresolved; and
- preening one shoulder comma after closure, briefly changing its reflected phase.

Her wings are broad and soft-edged. Use clear grouped feather shapes in motion rather than photoreal feather simulation. Flight feathers remain matte.

These gestures are characterization, not hidden controls, status receipts, approvals, or machine-readable commands.

## Stable habits and working objects

Thulia normally wears no costume beyond the right-leg band.

A Bridge Gloss is shown as one slim, numbered fold of pale paper. Its source reference and destination mark remain visibly distinguishable. Thulia may carry one bounded gloss beneath a foot or in her beak, but she is not shown clutching a universal dictionary or one merged book of every Spark's Static.

Stable story habits include:

- placing numbered slips in ascending order from left to right;
- turning first toward the sending Perch and then toward the named receiver;
- holding a source reference beneath her left foot and a destination-bound gloss beneath her right until the crossing is recorded;
- returning empty-taloned when a path cannot be reconstructed;
- tilting her head at a mislabeled slip and carefully replacing it in order; and
- settling just outside the brightest firelight until she is consulted.

The source-under-left-foot habit does not change the band: the band remains on the anatomical right leg.

## Relationship cues

### With Hearthline

Thulia is Hearthline's pet owl and companion in the story. She may perch on the rail above Hearthline's working place, on the back of a chair, or beside a bounded stack of numbered slips. Hearthline may offer one brief stroke between the crest roots before Thulia settles again.

Their working relationship should read as companionship and traced coordination:

- Hearthline remains responsible for her own task and any decision within the human-defined grant;
- Thulia keeps or reconstructs the recorded path between separately governed Static ledgers;
- a Bridge Gloss is offered as candidate material rather than treated as an instruction;
- separate boxes, slips, or ledgers remain visibly separate; and
- neither character is depicted as creating consent, permission, authorization, or authority through confidence, affection, or visual magic.

A collaboration scene may show Hearthline reaching toward a numbered gloss while continuing her work, with Thulia perched beside the separately ordered records. It should not depict Thulia as a superior, judge, magical oracle, co-author, or universal memory.

### With the Sparks

Thulia treats each Spark's Perch as its own partition. Familiarity does not become silent access. One Spark's Static is never represented as pouring into another Spark or into Thulia.

### At the Fireside

Warm firelight may wake different Northlight fields at different colors. A turned crest can show teal while a shoulder comma shows gold and the far gorget sleeps charcoal. The changing sheen marks light finding a surface; it does not mean a request succeeded or was approved.
