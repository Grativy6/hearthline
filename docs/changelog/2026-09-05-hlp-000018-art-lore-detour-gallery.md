  approved bridge composition: Gloss and Morrow character references; seven

## Why

The established stories had given Gloss and Morrow clear places in
Hearthline's world without giving their final visual designs a public home.
Creature art had likewise found a useful holographic language across water,
ground, and sky, but no written boundary separated a memorable animal-like
form from an operational Creature identity.

The new gallery keeps those choices together and exact-byte addressable while
letting art remain art. The counting story preserves the most important lesson
from the Gloss-sheet correction: sometimes the apparent extra mark is the
boundary required by the last interval, but a beautiful continuation is not
permission to draw what was never observed.

## Preserved boundaries

- The images, sheets, guide, and story are fictional presentation. They create
  no runtime, implementation, memory, identity continuity, personhood,
  capability, permission, standing, consent, authority, or external effect.
- Gloss's elastic depicted scale changes neither her identity nor the work she
  is allowed to do. The glassy graspers are a cool-contact visual cue, not a
  material specification, safety certification, tool interface, or new
  operational or physical capability claim.
- The ruler's `0` is a physical coordinate at its true endpoint. It is not an
  issued record ordinal. Ordered Hearthline identities continue to begin at
  `1`; seven ruler marks and six intervals answer different counting questions.
- One bounded frame expansion is warranted only when an independently checked
  origin, direction, interval, target, and evidence surface support the test.
  Pattern fit does not establish a missing mark, event, object, source, state,
  or continuation and does not license indefinite expansion.
- Morrow's appearance sheet changes no Return Queue rule. He remains
  deterministic, stateless, optional, and proposal-only. If the rail is absent
  or overturned, he stops; the controller's deterministic fallback preserves
  correctness.
- Morrow's near-white seam and eye light may dim or brighten as performance
  language. Deliberate hue-changing remains unspecified. Brightness is not a
  priority class, receipt, result, vote, permission, or status code.
- The queue-watch image depicts a controller-facing prototype. Its spark
  aperture is a transport metaphor: Hearthline does not allocate queue records,
  bypass readiness or committed order, open sealed payloads, or admit service,
  and Morrow does not gain those powers through the interface.
- Morrow and Thulia retain no channel, state, ledger, custody, invocation,
  impersonation, or availability dependency. A scene may connect controller
  machinery to controller machinery without connecting the characters.
- Creature-form names and visual scale do not define an animal species,
  manifest headcount, role, profile, task, priority, capability, budget, grant,
  memory, vote, authority, or permanent life. The controlling Creature design
  and a separately frozen manifest remain necessary.
- Prismatic light and enormous silhouettes may serve rule-of-cool composition.
  They make no claim about physics, projection technology, scientific evidence,
  or runtime behavior.
- Every earlier registered image keeps its identity and exact bytes. Rejected
  generations that never entered the repository receive no retroactive public
  identity and are not silently presented as adopted history.
- The first bridge-scene export is not represented as recovered. The registered
  bridge file is a new derivative from the visible composition, receives its
  own digest, and makes no byte-identity claim about the unavailable export.

## Compatibility and migration

This successor is additive and presentation-only. It does not change the
Moltbook candidate, source profile, Research Station, TETHER, Homecoming,
Return Queue, retry rotation, Creature mechanics, Sparks, Static, Firesides,
Thulia behavior, queue profile, service epoch, or operational record schema.

`GLOSS/SHEET-000001`, `MORROW/SHEET-000001`, and the
`CREATURE-FORM/IMAGE-*` series are first public presentation records. A future
appearance correction receives a new sheet or image ordinal rather than
overwriting these bytes. Creature-form identities remain disjoint from
`CREATURE-00000x` manifest identities.

The public Visual Index skips version `0.3` rather than adopting or rewriting
the unrelated off-main file. The permanently reserved HLP-000008 through
HLP-000013 records remain namespace evidence only; none of their content is
copied or silently endorsed by this release.

## Open residuals

- The selected images are visual references and scenes, not orthographic proof,
  animation rigs, production models, physical designs, accessibility studies,
  or tests of a deployed system.
- Complete generation prompts, serving configuration, and cryptographic
  certification of embedded provenance manifests remain unavailable and are
  not reconstructed.
- The queue-watch remains fictional presentation. Any real controller-facing
  interface, transport, record-minting flow, exception retrieval policy, or
  admission path would require a separate design, implementation, and
  authorization record.
- Creature forms have no automatic environmental access or motion. A future
  implementation must define and test any actual projection, sensing, movement,
  grant, controller, trace, and return behavior.
- The bridge-standoff derivative preserves the approved scene idea, not an
  exact first-export reconstruction. Any later exact or newly selected scene
  would receive a successor identity rather than overwrite this file.

## Evidence and exclusions

The public evidence consists of the exact selected PNG bytes, their dimensions
and digests, the written appearance and form boundaries, the fictional story,
structural checks, and Git history. It is not evidence that Gloss, Morrow,
Creatures, the queue-watch, a welding machine, or any depicted mechanism exists
or works.

No raw conversation, hidden reasoning, rejected generation, contact sheet,
private path or identifier, credential, queue state, task payload, benchmark
data, runtime trace, external authorization, or operational receipt is
included. AI systems assisted image generation, drafting, inspection,
adversarial review, repository preparation, and validation as tools; they are
not authors, co-authors, witnesses, operators, characters, or release
authorities.
