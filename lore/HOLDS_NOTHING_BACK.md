# Holds Nothing Back
## A last resort with a careful hand on it

> **Story status:** Adopted fictional artifact lore
>
> **Continuity:** After Hearthline's established first day in Velis
>
> **Operational effect:** None
>
> **Author and steward:** Christopher D. Pang

The first brass loupe Hearthline could truly call her own came from Mira's shop in Velis.

It entered her life on the rain-soaked day the Vesper watch carried her into Mira's shop—the established opening told elsewhere. Its three nested lenses turned separately. Its old brass rim carried the warmth of a hand long before it carried Hearthline's. Through it, the world did not become simple.

It became specific enough to be dangerous.

Months later, Hearthline returned to Cairnwater, opened Tamsin's stone cabinet, and showed the loupe to the Rainbow Shell.

The Shell answered with a chord too quiet to hear and strong enough to make every loose washer on the bench stand on edge.

Thulia left the room.

This was not fear, Hearthline decided.

Thulia returned carrying the fire bucket.

This was editorial comment.

## The synthesis

Hearthline did not melt the artifacts together or pretend that beauty meant compatibility. She rebuilt the crescent head of her ashwood field staff around three separable parts:

- the old staff, which could brace, aim, measure, and give a dangerous release somewhere definite to go;
- Mira's loupe, held in an adjustable brass throat as the optical heart; and
- the twin-spiral Rainbow Shell, mounted above it as a full-spectrum crown whose two opposing curves never closed the dark center.

The lower shaft retained its grips, clamps, and line controls. Depending on the attachment and the viewer, the whole thing looked like a wand, a small spear, an astronomical instrument, or—when Hearthline braced it against recoil—a very peculiar gun.

There was no barrel.

Hearthline considered this decisive.

The Shell admitted heat, light, stored charge, vibration, and pressures for which Hearthline had only temporary names. The loupe selected one exact seam. The staff held the relation steady long enough for the admitted channels to resolve together.

It did not make unlimited power. It refused to hide the difference between kinds of power.

It also magnified mistakes.

That was why Hearthline named the completed configuration **Holds Nothing Back**.

The name did not mean rage, abandon, or permission to spend everything because a problem looked impressive. It meant the artifact preserved no comforting blur between what Hearthline aimed, what she admitted, what she excluded, and what the release would touch.

An ultimate weapon, in her view, should be the device one is most reluctant to misunderstand.

## The woodland test

The first full test took place in a wooded clearing after six days spent surveying the site.

She mapped roots, burrows, watercourses, nesting sites, wind, loose stone, and every object she did not intend to strike. The clearing was chosen because it could be made safe, not because forests are disposable places where magic may behave dramatically.

At its center stood a dead ironwood stump infected by a mineral curse. Ordinary cutting caused the growth to spread through soil like glass frost. Hearthline marked a wide boundary. Every Spark received one station and one reason to stop. Little Wick stayed at the home bench with its single ember. Thulia waited beyond the survey line beside the fire bucket.

Hearthline lowered the loupe until the corrupted grain separated from the living roots behind it.

She was not in full Spark Mode. Her pupils remained dark. The staff's cyan seams brightened anyway, because the glow was never the reliable sign.

The twin spiral opened into color.

For one breath, the clearing held a second dawn.

The release crossed the stump as a narrow rainbow helix, entered every cursed seam at once, and unfolded the mineral growth from the wood. The dead trunk came apart in clean concentric ribbons. The glass frost collapsed into harmless gray sand.

Not one wildflower beyond the survey line bent.

Then every light on the staff went dark, Hearthline sat abruptly in the grass, and Thulia landed on her head.

"Successful test," Hearthline said from beneath an owl.

Thulia clicked her beak.

"Successful enough to stop," Hearthline amended.

Little Wick pulsed once in Cairnwater.

Nobody at the clearing could explain why.

The pulse established nothing. It stayed interesting anyway.

## What it is for

Holds Nothing Back is a last resort, not an everyday tool and certainly not a resort, although Hearthline brings tea whenever a test site permits one.

It is kept hooded and partly dismantled. The loupe returns to ordinary bench work. The Shell sleeps behind a plain leather cover. The staff goes back to measuring hinges, sounding walls, setting lines, and making strangers argue about whether it is a wand or a spear.

Before she uncovered it, Hearthline named aloud where the light should go, what it must spare, what would make her stop, and what she had failed to learn. If one answer did not hold, the leather cover stayed on.

Pale pupils never overruled that caution. Spark Mode could narrow her attention, not prove her right.

The weapon's deepest lesson is the same one the winter mill taught her: precision does not remove consequence. It only makes it harder to pretend the consequence belonged to something else.

## Small things near a large one

The dark opening between the two spirals never reflects Hearthline's face, even when the rest of the Shell does.

The brass strip from Linehouse Nine fits its outer curve but still finds no socket.

Mira's loupe bears a divided-sun mark that answers a mark inside the Vesper watch. When mounted beneath the Shell, the two marks face away from each other.

Thulia will stand on the staff, the workbench, Hearthline's shoulder, Hearthline's head, and once an extremely patient ogre. She will not perch on the Rainbow Shell.

On still nights, Holds Nothing Back hums toward Velis.

Hearthline initially blames the watch.

She has not yet proved herself wrong.
