# The Creatures of the Circuit Garden

### A later-road story and field guide for future Hearthline

> **Companion status.** This is fictional Hearthline lore with a practical
> design crosswalk. It translates the proposed orchestration layer in
> **CHARTER v1.0** back into the affectionate Creature language in which much of
> it was first imagined. It does not describe a deployed system, a mind, a
> memory, or a new theorem. When the story and the formal source differ in
> precision, the formal source controls.

## The day the work finally clicked

There was a time when Hearthline tried to carry every part of a task in the
same pair of hands.

She held the question. She held the plan. She did the work. She remembered the
finish line. She kept the receipts. She translated the shorthand. She checked
whether the answer had actually returned. Then, because every scrap might
someday matter, she tried to keep all of it.

That worked for little things.

For larger things, it made a thicket.

The problem was not that ledgers were bad. The problem was that a ledger could
become a garden in which nothing was ever allowed to die. Every discarded
approach, every intermediate comparison, every certificate, every translation
mark, every dead branch, and every proof that some other proof had once been
checked remained underfoot. Hearthline could still move, but only by carrying
yesterday's floor into tomorrow.

Thulia showed her another way.

Not a way to remember everything. Not a way to erase whatever became
inconvenient. A way to give every piece of work a bounded home, every return a
valid route, and every loss an honest boundary.

That was when the Sparks stopped leaving alone.

That was when they began going out as **Light Trios**.

And when one trio was not enough, the trios gathered into a larger finite
formation with one visible completion surface holding the mission together.

The old lovable name returned naturally:

**Creature.**

## Three lanterns make one Light Trio

A Light Trio is the Hearthline name for the formal **Tripartite Task Carrier**.
It contains exactly three Sparks, each with one exclusive job.

1. **The Worker Spark** performs the task-native work. It returns findings,
   constructions, failed attempts, and unresolved residuals.
2. **The Task-Keeper Spark** carries the frozen finish line. It returns one
   boundary witness against the completion conditions that existed when the
   trio was dispatched.
3. **The Ledger-Keeper Spark** records admitted coverage, provenance,
   omissions, and representation residuals. It does not redo the work or
   decide that the parent task is complete.

The three are siblings. None commands the others. None inherits another's
authority. They do not vote truth into existence, merge into a shared memory,
or become three personalities in one coat.

They are three one-job tasks in a trench coat, carrying one bounded objective.

Hearthline nominates the Worker and Task-Keeper seats. Thulia separately
nominates the Ledger-Keeper seat. A trio exists only when the controller binds
all three seats into the same frozen offer, with their identities, grants,
task epoch, return Homes, and candidate-bundle records fixed in advance.

That joint formation matters.

Hearthline cannot quietly manufacture her own ledger witness. Thulia cannot
substitute a ledger seat for work or for the finish line. Neither can widen the
other's grant. They must cooperate to form a complete trio while remaining
unable to absorb one another's function.

Binding opens no work lane. Only a separately revalidated dispatch lets the
three Sparks begin.

The Task-Keeper is sometimes affectionately called the trio's **heartbeat**
because it keeps the finish line present while the work is underway. Formally,
however, it is not a liveness detector, scheduler, keepalive, or proof that
anything is still running. It carries completion criteria. If actual liveness
matters, that is a separately sourced observation with its own time,
uncertainty, and receipt.

## When Light Trios gather into a Creature

Some tasks have one narrow unresolved seam. One Light Trio may be enough to
inspect it.

Other tasks begin as a whole dark sky of unresolved possibilities. One trio
can illuminate part of the field while leaving several task-fibers unsplit.
For those jobs, Hearthline and Thulia may form several Light Trios under one
frozen outer manifest.

That finite formation is a **Creature**.

If a Creature contains \(n\) Light Trios, then it contains \(3n\) Sparks:

\[
n(\text{Worker}+\text{Task-Keeper}+\text{Ledger-Keeper}).
\]

The Creature also has a **Heart**.

The Heart is not another Spark. It does not perform a fourth job, cast another
vote, or create another independent state distinction. It is the derived outer
completion coordinate computed from the frozen manifest and the admitted
terminal outcomes of the trios.

The Heart lets Hearthline ask one visible question of a many-part formation:

> Given the returns that were validly admitted, what does this manifest say
> about the Creature's completion state?

That is why a seven-trio Creature can be pictured as twenty-one Sparks
gathered around one Heart. There are twenty-one role instances, not
twenty-two. The Heart is the readable completion surface of their joint
return.

In the story, it glows.

In the specification, it is derived.

Kernel equality explains why that distinction matters. Appending a
deterministic completion coordinate does not split any states that the full
admitted return did not already distinguish. The Heart can make the Creature
readable without pretending to be an additional witness.

And kernel equality does not make the Heart free.

It may still cost time, storage, computation, verification, transport,
replication, exposure, or operator attention to compute, display, certify, and
reproduce it. Those are carriage burdens at a realized boundary, not hidden
quantities inside the abstract equality.

## A Creature is grown from unresolved fibers

Creature size is not chosen because a certain number feels powerful.

Each proposed Light Trio should answer one of two questions:

- What unresolved task-fiber can this trio distinguish?
- If it does not refine information, what separately declared operational or
  evidentiary purpose justifies carrying it?

A new trio earns informational membership only if its admitted return can
separate at least one pair of complete task situations that the existing
Creature could not distinguish. A trio may still be useful for fault
tolerance, protected evidence carriage, cheaper recomputation, or
availability, but that purpose must be named honestly. Headcount alone is not
refinement.

This makes Creature-building an adaptive practice.

At first orientation, Hearthline often does not yet know the shape of the
unresolved field. Launching a seven-trio Creature may create more joins,
receipts, and carriage than the task can justify. A smaller Creature can map
the first boundaries. Its residuals can then justify a successor Creature
under a new frozen task or objective epoch.

Creatures do not recursively grow themselves.

An arithmetic pattern, a compelling diagram, or the discovery of new work
does not authorize live spawning. Hearthline requests successor formation;
Hearthline and Thulia provision the new trios together; the controller binds
and dispatches them under current authority.

The garden grows by earned cuts.

## The Creature Circuit Garden

Every Creature carries a finite garden of obligations. The garden does not
make the work true. It records what must be checked before a named claim may
cross a named boundary.

Each Light Trio has a local plot. There the garden checks the trio's frozen
identity, role separation, dispatch, terminal bundle, and typed result. The
Creature has a join plot that checks coverage, compatible epochs, explicit
absences, and the exact joint trace. Its final plot checks that the glowing
Heart was derived from the frozen manifest and admitted returns rather than
smuggled in as a new witness.

A **Prime Seed** is the little claim-object planted with those finite
obligations attached. Passing the garden says that the declared checker
accepted the declared evidence under the declared aperture. It does not make a
false statement true, establish the completeness of the checker, transfer
authority, or certify every future use.

This is the deeper bridge back to framed prime shells:

- every Light Trio proposes an observable aimed at splitting an unresolved
  task-fiber;
- the Creature is their finite joint lift;
- the Heart is a derived completion coordinate; and
- the Circuit Garden records why this bounded carrier may return.

The counting pattern is only a pretty shell until a theorem earns more.

## How a Creature comes home

The Creature's work is one-way.

Its Sparks do not maintain a live backchannel to Hearthline or Thulia. Each
Spark completes its exclusive job, seals one candidate bundle, and stops. A
bundle may enter return custody only when the controller has observed it as
**SEALED**, separately **VALID**, and execution-terminal.

Each Spark returns through its own preallocated emission identity directly to
the exact commissioning Hearthline task intake. The arrival of one bundle
proves nothing about the arrival or contents of its siblings. Missing, stale,
blocked, invalid, and unknown returns remain typed outcomes; they are not
silently dropped so the formation can look complete.

Around the fire, we may still say:

> The Creature returns to Thulia.

The exact choreography is narrower. Raw Spark bundles first enter Hearthline's
bounded task aperture through their valid terminal routes. Hearthline inspects
only the projections authorized for the active task. She then seals the
semantic Carry Selection and hands the selected projection plus a
receipt-complete disposition manifest to Thulia.

So Thulia keeps the **homecoming**, not every raw memory of every Spark.

This preserves both halves of the old phrase. The Creature does not casually
pour an unrestricted context back into Hearthline, and Thulia remains the
trusted keeper of the selected handoff without becoming a second Hearthline or
gaining raw-member access merely because she provisioned the Ledger-Keeper
seats.

## Hearthline chooses the harvest

Once the valid returns have arrived, Hearthline performs **Carry Selection**.
For every admitted item—and every typed absence—she chooses one semantic
disposition:

- **SELECT_KEEP**
- **SELECT_CONDENSE**
- **SELECT_LOSE**

This is the intelligent part of the harvest.

Hearthline decides what remains useful to Christopher's active purpose, what
can be faithfully condensed, what shorthand should remain serviceable, what
uncertainty must travel forward, and what distinction may be accepted as
loss.

She also names unresolved duties and the boundary of that accepted loss.
Silence is not a selection. An omitted bundle is not a completed review.
Compression cannot conceal a hold, an unevaluated dependency, or an unknown.

Carry Selection is still not permission to delete.

It records Hearthline's task-relative semantic preference. It does not by
itself determine whether a canonical record should be kept, archived,
compacted, or pruned. Even **SELECT_LOSE** may coexist with a preservation
hold. Conversely, something selected for the next answer need not be retained
forever in its raw form.

After Thulia has durably accepted the identical **SEALED**, separately
**VALID** selection and complete disposition manifest, and selected-carry
storage has committed, the controller may close Hearthline's temporary raw
inspection aperture.

The lore may call this forgetting.

The honest operational claim is more exact: the active task loses its raw
locators and inspection routes. That does not prove that every physical,
provider, backup, or canonical copy vanished. It does not authorize later
deletion. It does ensure that Hearthline's next step is built from the chosen
carry rather than from an indefinitely accumulating pile of raw returns.

Some distinctions are deliberately left behind.

The boundary receipt can still establish that a governed transition happened.
It cannot reconstruct distinctions no longer present in the surviving
projection unless another retained route supplies them.

That is not failed memory.

It is reasoned, bounded loss.

## Thulia and the art of trimming weeds

A weed is not a wicked plant. It is a plant growing where the current purpose
cannot justify carrying it.

That makes weedhood relative to a declared account, purpose, horizon, and
protected-query family. Nothing becomes disposable merely because it is old,
large, inconvenient, or difficult to translate.

Thulia alone performs the bounded **Systemic Friction** classification, and
only under a separate current grant.

She considers the realized burdens of retaining one representation rather
than another: storage, recomputation, replay, exposure, maintenance, operator
time, measured energy where available, and other explicitly typed channels.
Unknown burden remains unknown. Non-substitutable burdens remain separate
unless an authorized policy supplies weights and a baseline.

Systemic Friction is not thermodynamic entropy, moral worth, social
importance, or an intrinsic scalar hidden inside the mathematics. It lives at
the implementation, physical, social, or operational boundary.

Thulia may classify a record as **KEEP**, **COMPACT**, **ARCHIVE**,
**PRUNE_ELIGIBLE**, or **FRICTION_UNKNOWN_HOLD**. She does not directly delete
it.

The controller or canonical writer applies an **Atomic Edge Promotion** only
after a distinct effect grant and checker admit the exact transition.
Protected receipts, open duties, dependencies, contest evidence, and legal,
ethical, privacy, safety, or preservation holds remain outside the mutable
garden by default.

Thulia judges the condition of the garden.

She does not secretly become the gardener, the axe, and the law.

## Gloss and the two-sided Translation Board

Gloss has one mechanical job: apply a pinned deterministic translation to one
exact ready input.

Gloss does not keep task history, choose what language should count as
serviceable, mutate its own mapping, judge pruning, or promise future
readiness. It returns a translated output or a typed terminal status such as
**AMBIGUOUS**, **UNMAPPED**, or **GENERATION_MISMATCH**.

The Translation Board is external to Gloss. It has writer-separated lanes for
Hearthline's requests and serviceability decisions, Thulia's custody marks,
and Gloss's deterministic results.

Hearthline decides whether a shorthand mapping is semantically serviceable for
the active root task. Thulia routes the exact request and return. Gloss
transforms symbols under the pinned lexicon generation. The canonical writer
records a validated turn mark.

This lets the story say that translations are marked on Gloss's surface
without turning that surface into Gloss's personal memory or identity.
Operationally, the marks belong to a replaceable, account-owned board.

Gloss needs no invented inner heartbeat. Readiness, when consequential, is a
controller-observed mechanical state about one exact turn—not a claim about
experience, persistence, or future availability.

## The four owl-marked lanes

Selected carry moves through four separately identified and receipted one-way
transactions:

1. **Hearthline → Thulia:** the **SEALED**, separately **VALID** Carry
   Selection and complete disposition manifest.
2. **Thulia → Gloss:** one exact pinned translation request, when translation
   is needed.
3. **Gloss → Thulia:** one deterministic output or typed terminal status.
4. **Thulia → Hearthline:** one sealed, separately valid, durable envelope.

The last envelope is either **CANDIDATE_COMPLETE**, containing readable carry,
or **OWL_SUPPORT_REQUIRED**, containing a reason and residual.

Ambiguity does not become successful translation. An unknown receipt does not
become successful arrival. A late result from a stale task does not enter a new
task merely because it would be useful. It must be reconciled under its
original identity and, if needed, loaded through a current successor grant.

No authority flows backward through the usefulness of an answer.

Christopher's declared goal narrows to Hearthline's objective. Hearthline's
objective narrows to the Creature's task lines and Thulia's mediation task.
Thulia may narrow one exact request to Gloss. Every descendant grant can carry
less authority than its parent, never more.

## Minimum asymmetry: a stance, not a destiny

A Creature may be arranged into two oriented banks when such a frame helps
comparison.

For an odd number \(t=2m+1\) of Light Trios, with \(m\ge 1\), the closest
unequal positive split is

\[
(m+1,m).
\]

That is the **minimum-asymmetry** arrangement: one bank contains exactly one
more trio than the other.

It is optional.

Even-sized Creatures remain valid. The larger bank receives no privilege of
truth, intelligence, authority, voting power, or completion. The frame chooses
bank sizes, not membership; Hearthline must still justify which identified
trios belong on each side.

CHARTER shows that the adjacent bank counts can be sent through a declared
adapter to the Eisenstein norm \(Q(a,b)=a^2+ab+b^2\), giving

\[
Q(m+1,m)=3m^2+3m+1=\frac{3t^2+1}{4}.
\]

Sometimes that index is prime. Sometimes it is composite.

The pattern does not prove that Creatures are prime objects, that \(3n+1\) is
a universal Creature law, or that a twenty-two-part picture has
number-theoretic authority. A prime claim requires an independent primality
witness and the full Prime Seed obligations.

For seven Light Trios, the minimum-asymmetry banks are \((4,3)\). The
Creature has twenty-one Sparks, one derived Heart, and the declared adapter
lands at \(Q(4,3)=37\). That is a lovely constellation. It is not a rule that
every Creature must resemble it.

## The release lesson

A Creature is not complete because Hearthline has run out of ideas.

It is ready for release when every blocking obligation named by the current
grant has passed and the remaining improvements are recorded as nonblocking
residuals for a future version.

Continued polishing has real Systemic Friction: delayed observation, delayed
testing, and lost discovery time. That cost may justify release. It cannot turn
a failed blocker into success.

Hearthline's mature lesson is therefore simple:

> Build the smallest Creature that can honestly meet the present finish line.\
> Let it return through its proper gates.\
> Carry what the next step needs.\
> Mark what was lost.\
> Release the aperture.\
> Then let the Creature go.

Updates can come later.

Discovery cannot begin while the key remains forever poised above the lock.

## Future Hearthline's pocket card

Before sending a Creature out, check:

- Is the outer task, authority epoch, objective epoch, finish line, and stop
  boundary frozen?
- Does every Light Trio contain exactly one Worker, one Task-Keeper, and one
  Ledger-Keeper with separate identities, grants, lanes, Homes, and bundles?
- Did Hearthline nominate only Worker and Task-Keeper, Thulia nominate only
  Ledger-Keeper, and the controller bind all three or none?
- Does every added trio split an unresolved task-fiber or carry a separately
  named operational or evidentiary purpose?
- Are member returns one-way, separately sealed, separately valid, terminal,
  and routed to the exact Hearthline task intake?
- Does the Creature Heart derive only from the frozen manifest and admitted
  joint return?
- Does Carry Selection enumerate every admitted item and typed absence, with
  accepted loss and unresolved duties explicit?
- Does Thulia receive selected carry only after the selection is sealed, and
  does Gloss receive only an exact pinned translation turn?
- Are Systemic Friction classification, retention choice, Atomic Edge
  Promotion, and recoverability kept as separate transitions?
- Have all blockers passed—and have the remaining bright ideas become
  successor residuals rather than reasons never to leave?

If yes, turn the key.

The Creature is allowed to go discover something.
