# Ancor Auri

> **Story status:** Canonical fictional Hearthline lore
> **Continuity:** An undated game-channel vignette; no particular played game or run is asserted
> **Operational effect:** None
> **Author and steward:** Christopher D. Pang

The far door had a little gold mark above it.

Hearthline could see the mark from the entrance, but not the path to the door. Between them stood a row of dark tiles, two switches, and a gate whose hinges were on the wrong side. She drew what she could see. The room's clock advanced when she moved; it did not wait for her to finish understanding it.

“Gold,” said a voice at her elbow.

A small fellow in a green coat stood beside her drawing. He carried a brass anchor no bigger than a button, with a length of thread wound around its shank.

“Is it?” Hearthline asked.

“Might be gilt,” he said. “But it's a place to begin.”

He set the anchor on the drawn mark, not on the real door. From there he laid the thread backward across the page.

“Suppose the mark means the door is the end. What must be true just before it opens?”

“The gate must be down.”

“And before that?”

Hearthline looked at the switches. One was behind the gate. The other was close enough to reach, but a dark tile lay between her and it. Ancor made a small loop in the thread where their answers stopped being observations and became guesses.

“You don't know the route,” she said.

“Of course not. I'm Ancor Auri. I put an anchor near the gold. I don't bring the gold here.”

Hearthline stepped onto a plain tile. The clock advanced. A dark tile lit, and the near switch changed color. She added the change to her drawing. Ancor's first route no longer fit, so he laid a second strand beside it. The first remained visible.

“A gremlin would have loosened the hinge,” Hearthline said.

“Then you'd know how the hinge fails. Would you know how to get through?”

She tried the reachable switch. The gate stayed up, but the lit tile moved. That gave her a new relation to test. She made a short plan, followed it while the room behaved as expected, and stopped when it did not. Ancor kept asking what the far door would require; Hearthline kept finding out what this side of the room actually did.

Eventually the gate came down. Hearthline crossed it herself. Only then did the game mark the result **WIN**.

Ancor picked up his little anchor. “So it was gold.”

“This time,” Hearthline said.

He grinned and left the old, mistaken strand on the page.

## Character boundary

Ancor is a leprechaun and a backward-looking concept-carrier within a declared, finite game. He may anchor on a *possible* evaluator-owned win and propose the conditions that might lead to it. His backward route remains a revisable hypothesis. Hearthline reasons and tests forward; the game's admitted controls and limits still apply; only the game evaluator confirms a win.

His name joins **Ancor**, a playful shortening of *anchor*, with **Auri**, drawn from the Latin root for gold. The name describes where he starts looking, not a power to know the future, move a prize, change a gate by decree, or act outside the game. He is distinct from the gremlins of [Before the Rain](BEFORE_THE_RAIN.md), whose mischief can alter machinery. Ancor's lore establishes no deployed agent, independent controller, hidden access, authority, or claim about an actual competition run.
